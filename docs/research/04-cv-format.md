# 4. CV format: structure, sections and typography

Research from October 2026. Main sources: the career guides of Harvard, MIT, Yale and Stanford; Workday, Greenhouse and Textkernel documentation; and sources from Latin America. Same confidence labels as in [01](01-how-ats-work.md).

---

## 4.1 The "Harvard format"

**It is not a single specification.** It is a set of templates and guides from Harvard's Mignone Center for Career Success (formerly the Office of Career Services): a bulleted template, a paragraph template and accessible Word versions (2025).
Sources: [Bulleted template](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/bullet-point-resume-template/) · [Accessible version](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/accessible-harvard-college-bulleted-resume-template/) — Primary.

**The student version** puts Education first: name, one contact line, then Education → Experience → Leadership → Skills. Each entry: organization, title, city and dates.
Source: [Harvard sample, 2026](https://cdn-careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/161/2026/07/resume-sample.pdf) — Primary.

**For experienced professionals the order changes.** The Harvard Extension School samples use **Summary → Core competencies → Experience → Education**, in about 2 pages. Its guide says education "can go at the end if experience is more relevant".
Source: [HES samples, 2026](https://cdn-careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/161/2026/02/HES-Resume-samples-combined.pdf) — Primary.

**Its form rules** (2026 guide): consistency, white space, and checking that formatting survives the conversion to PDF. **No:** narrative style, abbreviations, photo, age, gender, references, or lines that start with dates. Reverse-chronological order. No "I" or "we". Written for "people or systems that read quickly".
Source: [Harvard guide](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/) — Primary.

**Typography in Harvard's older guide (2021):** Times New Roman or Arial, 10–12 pt, margins of at least 0.75 inch, no text boxes, underlining or shading. The 2026 guide does not specify a typeface or margins.
Source: [Harvard master's guide, 2021](https://armeniseharvard.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/masters_resume_cover_letters.pdf) — Primary, old.

**Why is it called "ATS-friendly"?** Harvard does not market it that way; third parties added the label. It is ATS-friendly because it meets what ATS need: one column, standard headings, no graphics or tables, reverse-chronological order.
→ **Conclusion:** the Harvard format is a good base, but in its **professional version** (experience first), not the student one.

## 4.2 Chronological, functional or hybrid

**Executives prefer chronological:** 75 % chronological, 17 % functional (an old Accountemps survey of 150 executives).
Source: [Reliable Plant](https://reliableplant.com/Read/20200/75-of-execs-prefer-chronological-resumes-from-job-seekers) — Vendor, old.

**A functional CV (organized by skills, with no clear history) confuses ATS and raises suspicion.** Roche, in its careers FAQ: "a functional resume may confuse applicant tracking systems". Stanford and Virginia Tech say many employers do not like it.
Sources: [Roche](https://careers.roche.com/global/en/resume-parsing-faq) — Primary · [Stanford](https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/resume-and-cover-letter-examples.pdf) · [Virginia Tech](https://career.vt.edu/job-search/presenting_yourself/resumes/types_and_formats.html) — Primary.

**For career changers, the hybrid:** a summary and a skills block on top, then the full history in reverse-chronological order.
Source: [Jobscan, Sep 2026](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/recruiters-functional-resume-format/) — Vendor.
→ **Design choice:** the **hybrid** format: summary and skills on top, full reverse-chronological experience below.

## 4.3 Sections and their order

**General rule:** sections go in order of importance to the employer, and inside each one, from most recent to oldest.
Sources: [MIT, Apr 2026](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/) · Harvard — Primary.

**Standard headings.** Creative ones ("My journey", "My toolbox") get filed wrong; Greenhouse says unclear sections cause partial parses.
Sources: [Jobscan](https://Jobscan.co/blog/ats-formatting-mistakes/) · [Greenhouse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse).

**A professional summary, not an "objective".** The objective is outdated. The best CVs in the Ladders study had a summary at the top. Harvard (Sep 2026) recommends a short executive summary because it gets read more than a separate letter. **And Workday HiredScore uses the summary as one of the sections where it looks for evidence of each requirement.**
Sources: [Harvard, Sep 2026](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/blog/2026/09/15/resumes-for-industry-grad-student-edition-2026/) · [HiredScore, Aug 2026](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/hiredscore-release-notes/2026/august/wednesday--5th-august.html) — Primary.

**An explicit skills section.** Harvard and MIT ask for one; HiredScore looks for evidence in skills, languages and certifications. Write the level in text ("English — C1"), never as bars.
Sources: [Harvard on AI](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/ai-resumes-and-cover-letters/) · MIT · HiredScore — Primary.

**Certifications** with name, issuer, year and expiry. **Languages** with a single level framework (for example, the European A1–C2 scale). **No references** and no "references on request".
Source: [Yale](https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/resume-formatting/) — Primary.

**What recruiters filter on most** (384 respondents, 2025): skills 76 %, education 60 %, job title 55 %, certifications 51 %, years of experience 44 %, location 43 %.
Source: [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/interview-rates-study) — Vendor.

## 4.4 How to write each bullet

**Laszlo Bock's XYZ formula (former head of people at Google):** "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]." Harvard recommends a variant: Project → Action → Result. If there is no number, use a word that conveys value.
Sources: [Business Insider on Bock](https://www.businessinsider.in/googles-former-hr-boss-says-this-is-the-key-to-a-perfect-resume/articleshow/54925993.cms) — Anecdotal · [Texas A&M–Commerce](https://www.etamu.edu/bullet-point-formula-sheet/) — Primary.

**Rules from Yale, MIT and Stanford:**
- Start with an action verb. Past tense for previous roles; simple present for the current one. Never gerunds, never "Responsible for" or "Duties included".
- 1–2 lines per bullet. Fragments, not full sentences. No pronouns.
- Quantify size, scale, budget or people managed.
- Yale caps bullets at 3–4 per role, but that is a student norm; Harvard uses more for experienced profiles.
Sources: [Yale](https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/resume-formatting/) · [MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/) · Stanford — Primary.

**On honesty and AI:** Bock: lying is "never, ever, ever worth it". Harvard: "generative AI should not be the primary author"; it recommends building a "bank" of experiences to draw material from.
Sources: [WEF, Bock 2014](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2014/12/the-biggest-mistakes-google-sees-in-cvs/) · [Harvard on AI](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/ai-resumes-and-cover-letters/) — Primary.
→ This is exactly how CV Factory works: one verified master profile that every CV is drawn from.

## 4.5 Typography and layout

| Source | Typeface | Size | Margins | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MIT | Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman | 10–12 pt | 0.5–1 in | 1 page; 2 with a graduate degree or 10+ years |
| Yale | Times New Roman, Helvetica, Garamond | 10–12 pt (name 12–14) | ≥ 0.5 in | Black and white |
| Bock (Google) | — | ≥ 10 pt | ≥ 0.5 in | "White paper, black ink" |
| Roche | Arial, Helvetica, Times, Garamond, Georgia, Cambria | — | — | Write URLs in full; color gets lost |
| DaXtra (CV parser) | Aptos, Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman | — | 1 in | One column |
| Jobscan | — | 10–12 pt | 1 in (minimum 0.5) | Left-aligned, not justified |

Sources: [MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/) · [Yale](https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/resume-formatting/) · [Roche](https://careers.roche.com/global/en/resume-parsing-faq) · [DaXtra](https://info.daxtra.com/blog/passing-the-parser-how-to-get-your-resume-seen-by-recruiters-in-2024) · [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/how-to-set-resume-margins/).

**No study shows that one typeface beats another.** The real rule: a standard, embeddable typeface, so the text extracts cleanly. Unusual fonts and icons come out as "Pro?le" or "[NULL]".

**What makes parsing fail, according to Greenhouse** (updated Mar 2026): tables, headers and footers, **name or contact details in the header, footer or a text box**, columns, graphics or photos, image files, **letter-spaced text**, unclear sections, **abbreviated titles** ("Sr. Account Exec") and files over 2.5 MB.
Source: [Greenhouse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse) — Primary.

**Dates with the month's name** ("January 2021 – March 2023"; the engine uses the full month name because OpenResume does not recognize "Jan", see [05 §5.6](05-generating-pdf-and-docx.md)). Never numbers only (day/month and month/day get confused between the US and Latin America) and never the year alone for jobs (education and certifications may show only the year). Badly written dates break the years-of-experience calculation, which is a common filter.
Source: [Jobscan, Sep 2026](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/resume-dates) — Vendor.

**An important contradiction about where dates go:** Jobscan recommends right-aligning them with a tab. But in our own technical tests (see [05](05-generating-pdf-and-docx.md)), dates right-aligned with a tab, a table or flexible spacing **came apart from the job title** in two of the most widely used readers. Dates on the same line, after a bar, survived in all of them.
→ **Design choice:** dates on the same line as the title: "Claims Specialist, Example Services | October 2025 – Present".

**Length:** Bock: one page per ten years of experience. MIT: 1 page, 2 with a graduate degree or more than 10 years. ResumeGo: for mid-level or management experience, recruiters preferred two pages.
→ **Design choice:** 1 page with less than about 5–7 years of experience; 2 at most after that. Never 3.

## 4.6 Latin America vs. the US

**US and international:** no photo, age, gender, marital status, religion or references (Harvard, Yale, Stanford, Roche).

**Latin America — example: Costa Rica.** Article 404 of the Labor Code bans discrimination based on age, sex, marital status, origin, religion, disability and other grounds. Local experts recommend a photo "only if requested" and leaving out marital status and home address.
Sources: [La República, 2018](https://www.larepublica.net/noticia/discriminacion-en-el-trabajo-cuales-han-sido-los-alcances-de-la-reforma-procesal-laboral) · [Observador, Mar 2025](https://observador.cr/esta-buscando-empleo-o-una-nueva-oportunidad-laboral-experta-da-5-consejos-para-ser-atractivo-a-los-reclutadores/) · [Delfino, 2022](https://delfino.cr/2022/08/curriculum-con-foto) — Anecdotal.

**Latin America — example: Chile (Universidad Andrés Bello, 2024):** no photo, address, date of birth or national ID number; no tables or columns; Arial, Calibri or Times at 9–12 pt; a PDF named with first and last name.
Source: [UNAB guide](https://alumni.unab.cl/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/20240419_ManualDelBuenCVUNAB.pdf) — Primary.

**No serious source was found saying that private-sector employers in the region expect a photo, national ID or date of birth** on a CV. It is an old habit. The public sector uses its own forms.
→ **Design choice:** photo, national ID and nationality are **off by default**, used only if the company asks. Never marital status, age or children. **One language per document**: the language of the job description.

## 4.7 For AI agents

- Workday HiredScore with AI looks for evidence in **experience, education, skills, languages, certifications and summary**: every requirement has to be written in one of those standard sections. [HiredScore](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/hiredscore-release-notes/2026/august/wednesday--5th-august.html)
- Textkernel's AI parser admits it can make up data, accepts files up to about 3 MB and does not return each job's bullets. [Textkernel](https://developer.textkernel.com/tx-platform/v10/resume-parser/overview/llm-parser/)
- **One verifiable claim per bullet**, so a model can quote it as evidence (our own recommendation, based on how HiredScore and LinkedIn work).
